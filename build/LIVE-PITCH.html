The 12-year-old pitch

Imagine your bakery has a robot helper.

It remembers every cake you sold, every naira that came in, every bag of flour in the store, and every cake still in the oven. It also stops you from accidentally selling a cake you don't have, or letting the wrong person change the prices.

That's this app.

It is basically a bakery video game – except the cakes are real and the money matters.

You open the dashboard and see how much money came in today, how much you still need to collect, which cakes are running low, what needs baking, what you spent, and whether your stock numbers actually match reality.

You click New order, pick a customer, add a cake, take a deposit, and the app handles the rest: the receipt number, the balance, the stock, the status, the history, and the report at the end. The calendar shows what is due each day. The delivery board shows what is ready, who is taking it, and where it is going. The payments page is the money jar. The documents page is the filing cabinet. The quotes page keeps the maybes from getting mixed up with confirmed work.

Need to send a list to your accountant or keep a copy? The main working lists can be downloaded as CSV, Excel, Word or PDF, or printed on paper. You are never trapped inside the screen.

The important bit: this is not a pretty page sitting in front of a spreadsheet. The rules live in the database. The screen is the shop counter; the database is the warehouse manager, security guard and accountant rolled into one.

And because it is a bakery, not a toy, there is a boss mode. The owner sees money, reports, staff, settings, audit logs and the recycle bin. Staff can take orders and do the day-to-day work, but cannot wander into the owner's office.

Who handles what

Every job has one clear owner. This is the part that keeps the app honest when the screen, the network or a person makes a mistake.

JobHandled byWhat that means in the bakery
The front doorSupabase AuthChecks the email and password, creates the session and sends password-reset emails. No custom password store is kept in the app.
Who can do whatPostgres Row Level Security, plus interface checksThe owner gets the office keys. Staff get a working badge. The restriction is enforced by the database, not just by hiding a button.
The memorySupabase PostgresCustomers, orders, payments, products, ingredients, recipes, stock movements, expenses and settings all live in one live database.
The rule robotSQL functions and triggersRecalculates totals, validates discounts, writes status history, applies stock movements and blocks edits to records that must stay frozen.
Money mathsPostgres numeric(14,2) and total triggersPrices, deposits, balances and refunds are calculated from the saved records – not trusted from a number typed into the screen.
Saving an ordercreate_order() database functionSaves the order and all its cake lines together. If one part fails, the whole order fails. No half-saved sales.
Stock controlAppend-only stock ledger and triggersA one-way CCTV tape. A completed order deducts stock once; a cancellation reverses it once. Every movement remains visible.
Receipts, quotes and invoicesissue_document() snapshotsCreates a numbered document with the prices at that moment. Later price changes cannot rewrite an issued document.
Payments ledgerPayments table and recalc_order_paid() triggerEvery deposit, balance payment and refund updates the order balance. The payments page shows the full history and outstanding debtors.
Calendar and deliveriesOrders with due dates and delivery detailsThe calendar groups work by due date. The delivery board groups it by run date, address and rider, and moves the order from ready to dispatched to delivered.
Errors and validationFriendly app messages, plus database constraintsThe interface explains problems in plain language. The database is the final referee and refuses invalid writes even if the interface is bypassed.
Files and receiptsSupabase Storage, private bucket cake-filesStores expense receipts and order attachments. The app creates short-lived signed links instead of exposing files publicly.
Mailing and messagingSupabase Auth email; WhatsApp links; print and PDF layoutsSupabase handles account and password-reset emails. Quotes and invoices are shared by WhatsApp, printed, or downloaded as PDF. The app does not automatically email invoices and has no separate mail server.
Reports and exportsSQL views, JavaScript tables, CSV, Excel, Word, PDF and printThe scoreboard for sales, profit, cash-up and restocking. Every list opens an export chooser so the same data can become a spreadsheet, a Word document, a PDF or paper.
NotificationsDatabase triggers and app refreshLow-stock and attention alerts are written as notification records and refreshed in the app. They are not push notifications to a phone.
Audit trail and recycle binAudit triggers and soft deleteThe diary records who changed money or stock. Deleted records go to trash first, so mistakes can be restored before they are purged.
Hosting and deliveryStatic files plus SupabaseThe HTML, CSS and JavaScript can be served by any static host. Supabase supplies the database, login, storage and security.

What is now live-only

The project has been stripped back to the real system. There is no local pretend database and no sample business to explore.

AreaCurrent behaviour
DatabaseThe app connects only to Supabase. If the URL or anon key is missing, it shows setup instructions instead of opening a local fallback.
AccountsThe first account can claim ownership once from the sign-in page. Later accounts start as staff, and an owner promotes them when ready. The database refuses a second owner claim.
Business dataThe database starts with the settings row and no customers, products, orders or fake history. The owner builds the real catalogue.
Removed toolingThe browser database, local session store, sample data file, sample accounts and reset button have been removed.
Kept for confidenceThe SQL regression suite and the stock-integrity check remain. They test the real database and roll back their own fixtures.

How to go live

  1. Create a Supabase project.
  2. Run supabase/schema.sql, then supabase/002_core_additions.sql, then supabase/003_owner_setup.sql in the SQL editor.
  3. Put the project URL and anon public key in js/config.js.
  4. Serve the folder with node tools/serve.js and open http://localhost:5173.
  5. Choose Create owner account on the sign-in page. The first account becomes the owner. If email confirmation is on, confirm the email and sign in; the claim finishes automatically.
  6. Add staff by letting them sign up normally, then promote them in Admin → Users & roles.
  7. In Supabase Authentication → URL Configuration, set the Site URL and add your local and production addresses.
  8. Confirm the private storage bucket cake-files and its storage policies exist.
  9. Build the real product, ingredient, recipe and customer catalogue, then start taking orders.

Honest boundaries

  • No automatic invoice email. Supabase sends account and password-reset emails. Quotes and invoices are shared by WhatsApp, printed, or downloaded as PDF.
  • No scheduled cloud backup. The owner can download a full JSON snapshot from Admin → Data & integrity. Database backups are controlled in Supabase.
  • No phone push notifications. Notifications are database records refreshed in the app.
  • No offline write queue. The app warns when the connection is down; writes need the live database.
  • Uploads need the private storage bucket. The schema sets up the database side; confirm the cake-files bucket and storage policies exist in the Supabase project.
  • PDF and Word need the document engine once. If the CDN is blocked, the app prints instead. The data is not uploaded anywhere.

In one sentence: Cake Slice Factory is a live bakery control room – the screen takes the order, Supabase remembers it, and the database rules make sure the cake, the cash and the stock all tell the same story.
